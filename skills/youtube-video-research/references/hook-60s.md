# The first 60 seconds

YouTube Studio's "Intro" key moment is the share of viewers still watching at 0:30. A high number means the opening matched what the title and thumbnail promised; the fix for a low one is a new title, thumbnail or opening. There is no universal benchmark: Studio compares the video with the channel's last 10 of similar length. Widely shared creator production guides call 0:00 to 1:00 the most important minute: prove the promise, then stop telling people what they will watch and start showing them. For business channels: at most 3 short lines, repeat the title's promise, make the viewer more curious than the packaging did, then go straight to point 1.

## Read the author's last openings first

Before writing, pull the captions of the author's last 3 or 4 videos and fill this table. It shows what their openings actually do.

| Video | 0:00 to 0:15 | 0:15 to 1:00 | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| <title, views a day> | <what they said and showed> | <where the payoff started> | <what to keep or cut> |

Count filler words ("essentially", "actually", "basically") and words spoken in the first minute; that sets the author's pace.

Outlier openings in most niches run about 200 to 270 words in the first 60 seconds and open on 1 of 4 moves: the proof object on screen (the list, the dashboard, the result), the promise with a number and a contrast, the belief the video breaks, or the wave named and dated ("It came out on Tuesday"). Few open on a disclaimer or on other creators.

## The 5 beats

About 160 words in total; up to about 220 if the take stays clear. Each beat names what is on screen.

| Beat | Time | Words | Job | Screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Confirm | 0:00 to 0:05 | up to 15 | Say the title's promise as an outcome. No greeting, no name | The thumbnail's hero object, live |
| 2 Stakes | 0:05 to 0:15 | up to 30 | Why now: the wave and the gap ("X is everywhere, nobody uses it for Y yet") | The wave: the launch page, an outlier, the logo |
| 3 Receipt | 0:15 to 0:25 | up to 25 | The author's proof in 1 line, cleared numbers only, and who they are in half a line | The real number on the real screen |
| 4 Loop | 0:25 to 0:45 | up to 50 | What the viewer gets, in 3 parts, with 1 open loop on the most valuable part ("the 3rd step is the one that brought the results") | A 3-part card or the map |
| 5 Payoff | 0:45 to 1:00 | up to 45 | Start part 1 now. No "let's hop into it" | Step 1 on screen |

## Rules

- The first sentence uses the title's words; the first frame shows the thumbnail's object. The opening is part of the packaging.
- Show before you explain: the result first, the how after.
- No benchmarks, history or theory before the first payoff. Move them after 1:00 or cut them.
- The subject is the viewer's outcome and the author's run, not other creators.
- Cut the author's filler words from the script.
- No subscribe ask, no channel intro, no sponsor in the first minute.
- In the first 30 seconds the picture changes every 3 to 5 seconds (screen, zoom, card, B-roll); mark the cuts in the script for [longform-edit](../../longform-edit/SKILL.md).
- Re-engage around 3:00 with a new loop or a surprising result.

## Measure

After the upload, log the share at 0:30 and 1:00 in the ledger and compare it with the channel's typical retention in Studio. A script that beats the last 10 at 0:30 stays the template; one that does not gets a new beat 1.
