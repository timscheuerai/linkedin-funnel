# Repurpose content

Read `AGENTS.md`, relevant identity, audience, strategy and voice pages, and the full supplied source. For a video, work from the final-cut transcript, so timestamps match what viewers see. Use `capture-context` if the source has not been preserved. Record source sections or timestamps for each candidate.

## Reuse the approved argument

When the source already has an approved long piece (in a content waterfall, the newsletter written from the video), that piece is the argument every derivative reuses. Adapt it per platform; do not re-derive the argument from the transcript for each piece. The transcript is for harvesting ideas the newsletter did not carry.

## Harvest the ideas

Read the whole source and tag every idea by type:

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| Chapter | A section that teaches 1 thing end to end |
| Framework | A named model, sequence or list the reader can copy |
| Proof moment | A result, screenshot or before and after shown in the source |
| Strong claim | A position someone could disagree with |
| Line | A quotable sentence |
| Receipt | A screenshot or cleared number |
| Take | 1 sharp opinion |

Ask of each: does it stand on its own without the source? Yes: it becomes a piece. No: it goes to a backlog with its timestamp for a later week, or is dropped.

Route each standalone idea to the format that carries it:

- Chapter or framework: a post that teaches it (`linkedin-copywriter`, `x-copywriter`), or a graphic when the relationship is clearer visually (`graphics-designer`, `flowchart`).
- Proof moment or receipt: a proof post with the screenshot or before and after.
- Strong claim or take: an opinion post.
- Line, receipt or take on X: a micro post planned with `x-planner`.
- A clip worth cutting: a short with its platform copy from `shortform-platform-copy`.

Rank candidates by usefulness to the intended reader and fit with the author's strategy, not by how many posts can be produced. Different wording alone does not make a second distinct piece.

## Shape each piece

For each selected piece, identify one point, its source section or timestamp, the pillar and the funnel job (Reach, Belief or Signup) before drafting; the job sets the hook and the ask. Preserve the source's uncertainty, time period and scope. Do not transfer a guest's experience into the author's first-person story.

A request for a video brief can produce a sourced storyboard and shot list; do not claim a rendered video without actually rendering and inspecting it.

Keep the CTA aligned with the current offer. Mention the original source when it helps the reader (for a video, the full `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>` URL, plus `&t=<seconds>s` to point at a chapter), rather than opening every piece with an announcement about repurposing. A chapter that answers a buyer objection can later be sent as that answer in a DM.

## Deliver

Deliver the requested quantity the source supports and explain any shortfall. Include a compact source-to-piece map outside the copy: idea, type, timestamp, format, funnel job, and backlog items with the reason they wait. Save to Notion only within the requested scope using the configured destination and existing-card check; in a waterfall each piece is a sub-post of the video's big post, never a separate card. Drafting does not publish or schedule anything.
